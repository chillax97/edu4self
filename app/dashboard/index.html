Two minutes: one idea, one question

Welcome to edu4self

Here you learn by pulling things out of your head, not by reading them again. Try it once: read one idea, then answer from memory.

The idea

Pulling an idea out of your head is what makes it stay. Reading it again makes it feel familiar, and familiar feels like knowing. It isn't.

In a classic study, students who reread a passage did a little better five minutes later. A week later, the ones who had tested themselves remembered clearly more.

Roediger and Karpicke, 2006

How edu4self works

  1. Look at Up next. It's always the single most useful thing to do right now.
  2. Open it and do it. Lessons open in your browser; notes open as text, or in Obsidian if you turn that on in Settings.
  3. Submit your answers with the button on the card.
  4. Press ✦ Grade it now. Claude grades it in the page. (No Claude button? Install Claude Code, or say "check my inbox" in Claude Code from your vault folder.)
  5. Come back here. Claude will have graded it and lined up what's next.

What things mean

Lesson What's a lesson?

A short page that teaches one idea and ends with exercises. It only counts as done once Claude has checked your answers, not when you reach the bottom.

Probe What's a probe?

A set of questions that measures what you already know. It teaches nothing. Claude uses it to skip what you know and aim at exactly what you don't, which is why it only works if you do it unaided. "I don't know" is a perfectly good answer.

Checkpoint What's a checkpoint?

A probe that closes a milestone. Pass it and the milestone is complete.

What are milestones?

Big, observable abilities, written as something you can do, not something you have read: "can explain X to a friend without notes", "can build Y from a blank file". Claude sets them with you when a topic starts. The estimates are in lessons, not days, and they get revised as real results come in.

RED What's the readiness gate?

Optional, for topics that end in a high-stakes attempt you can't retake freely: an exam, a certification, an interview. The gate says whether to spend that attempt yet. RED: hold off. AMBER: close, keep drilling with realistic mocks. GREEN: you've passed realistic mocks, so go for it. Topics without a gate don't show one.

What's the skills ladder?

Every area moves up four rungs: heard of it → understand it → can use it on my own → have public proof. Rungs only go up when there's evidence, never just because a lesson was read.

What's the evidence log?

A square lights up on days you proved something: passed a check, transferred an idea to a new problem, solved something unaided. Reading doesn't light it. It's a record of what you've demonstrated, not of time spent.

The bar view and the classic view

Same dashboard, two looks. The button at the top switches between them, and this browser remembers your choice; Settings sets the default. In the bar, your skills talk: each voice comments on something real, like tonight's minutes, your die, what's up next or a past win. Some lines are written by Claude after grading. Press 1 to 9 to pick a reply. The back room holds milestones, proof, skills, your library and the die case. Click the neon sign to cycle the bar's name. Names, voices, your own lines and the jukebox track are all in Settings.

Water rounds

While a block runs, a glass of water arrives every 15 minutes: a soft clink, and a notification if the dashboard isn't the tab you're looking at. The timer keeps going. Press Drank it (or click the glass in the bar) when you have. Set it to 15, 30 or off before you start a block. Each block saves how many glasses came and how many you drank. Nothing nags you about the ones you skipped.

The die, the die case, and why minutes count

Every finished focus block is laid end to end with the others and cut into 25-minute tiles. 24 tiles etch one die (10 hours). 8 dies make a row, 4 rows a tray. It is a record of time you actually focused, not of time you meant to.

Privacy

Everything runs on this computer. The dashboard saves your submissions to _system/inbox.jsonl, your blocks to _system/blocks.jsonl and your settings to _system/config.json, all inside your vault. Nothing is sent to the internet. Claude only reads the inbox when you ask it to.

Troubleshooting

The page won't load

If you installed it as a service, it starts when you log in. To restart it, run this in a terminal from the edu4self folder:

./edu4self restart

./edu4self doctor checks everything and says what's wrong.

I submitted the wrong thing

As long as it's still waiting for Claude, press Withdraw on the card and submit again.

How do I add a new topic?

Press + New topic at the top and say what you want to learn. Claude asks a few questions, sets up the folder, and the topic appears here. (Or, in Claude Code from your vault folder, say "I want to learn" and the topic, or run /edu4self:new-topic.)

How do I turn it off? 
./edu4self uninstall

Your files stay. ./edu4self install brings it back.

Settings

Make it yours. Changes apply when you press Save. They're kept in _system/config.json in your vault, which you can also edit by hand.
