# Answer key: hlw-lesson-01

For how-learning-works/lessons/0001-retrieval-beats-rereading.html. Written with the example topic.

## 1. Retrieval strength vs storage strength

Required (each one must be in the answer, in any words):
- 1a. Retrieval strength is how easily something comes to mind right now.
- 1b. Storage strength is how deeply it is learned: how well it survives time.

Wrong answers to watch for (stated as fact, each is an error):
- The two are the same thing, or one simply grows with the other: rereading raises retrieval strength while storage strength barely moves.
- Storage strength is about where or how much you wrote down (notes, files): it is about the memory.

Not required (never fail an answer for leaving these out):
- The Bjorks' names, the term "desirable difficulty", the experiments.

## 2. Why rereading feels better while helping less

Required:
- 2a. Rereading makes the material familiar and easy to bring to mind right now (it raises retrieval strength), and that ease feels like learning.
- 2b. It does little for long-term memory (storage strength), so the feeling of knowing is a poor guide to what will be remembered later.

Wrong answers to watch for:
- Rereading doesn't work at all, or harms memory: it helps a little, and helps most in the very short term.
- You feel confident because you really did learn it: the confidence comes from familiarity, not recall.

Not required:
- Roediger and Karpicke's numbers, the five-minute vs one-week result, the Dunlosky review.

## 3. One concrete way to practise retrieval this week

This question has no single right answer: it asks for a plan of the learner's own.

Required:
- 3a. A specific subject the learner is learning or wants to.
- 3b. A concrete action in which they produce the material from memory before looking (closed-book questions, writing down what they remember, explaining it aloud, flashcards answered before turning), not one where they look at it again.

Wrong answers to watch for:
- A plan that is rereading, highlighting, rewatching or summarising with the material open: that is review, not retrieval.

Not required:
- Spacing, a schedule, a number of sessions, a tool.
